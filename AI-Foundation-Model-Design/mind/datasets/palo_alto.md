# Palo Alto Networks

Palo Alto Networks is a cybersecurity company known for its next-generation firewalls and the PAN-OS operating system.
PAN-OS is the operating system that runs on Palo Alto Networks firewalls.
A Palo Alto firewall organizes interfaces into security zones, such as trust and untrust, and policies control traffic between zones.
A security policy on a Palo Alto firewall allows or denies traffic based on zones, addresses, applications, and users.
App-ID is the Palo Alto technology that identifies the actual application in traffic regardless of port or protocol.
User-ID is the Palo Alto technology that maps network traffic to specific users for identity-based policies.
Content-ID is the Palo Alto technology that scans traffic for threats, malware, and sensitive data.
A security profile on a Palo Alto firewall applies antivirus, anti-spyware, vulnerability protection, and URL filtering to allowed traffic.
WildFire is the Palo Alto cloud service that analyzes unknown files in a sandbox to detect new malware.
URL Filtering on a Palo Alto firewall controls which websites users can access by category or address.
Panorama is the Palo Alto product that centrally manages many firewalls from a single console.
GlobalProtect is the Palo Alto VPN that gives remote users secure access to the network.
Prisma Access is the Palo Alto cloud-delivered SASE service that secures remote users and branch offices.
Prisma Cloud is the Palo Alto platform that secures applications and workloads across public clouds.
Cortex XDR is the Palo Alto product that detects and responds to threats across endpoints, network, and cloud.
Cortex XSOAR is the Palo Alto security orchestration, automation, and response platform.
A zone protection profile on a Palo Alto firewall defends against floods, reconnaissance, and packet-based attacks.
The application command center, or ACC, gives a visual summary of activity and threats on a Palo Alto firewall.
A virtual system, or vsys, splits one Palo Alto firewall into several independent virtual firewalls.
Single-pass architecture means a Palo Alto firewall inspects a packet once for all functions, which keeps it fast.
